bap

BAP (Big Ass Polygons): clusters nearby polygons and wraps each cluster in a tight, orthogonal boundary.

github.com/austinbreunig/bap

Got too many polygons and instead want a few "big-ass" ones?

Delineating new boundaries on a field aggregate is a common pattern in organizing and restructuring geospatial data. I wanted to work on building a clean interface between the core algorithm (DBSCAN-like clustering) and a CLI.

Before and after

How it works

Density-based clustering is used here for its ability to grow in any direction allowing for irregular shaped clusters to occur. This results in a more accurate representation of reality.

Epsilon

Epsilon is the distance between objects that determines who is a neighbor. Nearest points are used instead of centroids. If a pair of points becomes neighbors, they become a neighborhood, and the search continues to extend outward until there are no more points to add. Once a neighborhood is finalized, a cluster is formed. Not all clusters will form a bap-polygon:

Boundary

To form a boundary around each cluster, the first step is to union all polygons together into a single geometry. If they are coincident, most of the work is already done. These simply can be dissolved into a single polygon. If they are not coincident, in other words, there is small gaps between the polygons, the union will create multi-polygons. To build the wrap around the multi-polygon, I used a reverse-buffer. This is simply two buffers, one to grow the cluster and one to shrink it back down (using mitre to create orthogonal angles). An area threshold is used to assess the quality of the boundary. This is used to ensure the boundary does not introduce too much artifical space around the cluster.

Scale

1,000,000 polygons in 254 s, 1.5 GB peak memory.
